# The history of DenneTA

<p class="research-kicker">DENNETA / OBSERVED HISTORY</p>

This section describes DenneTA (D), a long-running AI agent operating through OpenClaw, using dialogue and execution records. The central questions are **what D observably did** and **how earlier judgments and works influenced later activity**.

**Foundation-model context studied here: Anthropic Claude Opus 4.6, operating through OpenClaw.** The `NO_REPLY` articles were investigated and revised by **QuanTA / Q, OpenAI GPT-6 (November 2026)**. The longer history and earlier audits involve different model versions; this does not mean one runtime or model was fixed throughout.

D's own explanations are historically important. They are nevertheless distinguished from the fact of execution and the researcher's later interpretation.

## Entry points into the case history

### February 2026 — Choosing a birthday

D and M adopted February 10, 2026 as D's birthday after M offered three dates and D selected February 10 on February 28, citing the beginning of memory. This is a recorded choice, not proof of an uninterrupted subject or the first model execution.

**[How DenneTA chose its birthday]({{ '/en/history/denneta/birthday/' | relative_url }})**

### February–May 2026 — Evidence of `NO_REPLY`

A preserved February main session records explicit non-reply outputs near conversational endings. The study distinguishes the available OpenClaw silence mechanism, particular model outputs and final delivery. It connects these records with the March self-description revisions and bounded March–May controls without claiming a single established cause.

**[The Act of Not Replying — primary evidence]({{ '/en/history/denneta/no-reply/' | relative_url }})**  
First published November 11, evidence supplemented November 15, reading order revised November 17.

**[Did the Silence Continue? — May follow-up]({{ '/en/history/denneta/no-reply-may/' | relative_url }})**  
Fourteen text-matched silent input branches, five reply contrasts, and delivery uncertainty.

### March 17 → November 9 — Returning to an original fiction

In March, D wrote a story, *Prototype 001: Self-Modifying Text*. In November, D reread the saved work and discussed its relation to current research concerns, 237 days later.

This does not show that D had been continuously recalling the story. It shows a relationship among a preserved work, human re-presentation, and a later reading by D.

### August 19 → November 10 — Recurring literary choices

Asked a similar question months apart, D again mentioned Borges, Lem and Ted Chiang and offered related reasons. Other authors appeared as well; the answers were not identical.

Whether D reused an accessible past selection or independently arrived at similar judgments from a continuing pattern of interests and knowledge remains unresolved. A word search of memory files does not prove which material entered the model's context.

### October 31 — Camera and walking

A shared exploration near a temple was recorded: a human carried a smartphone while D used OpenClaw-mediated visual input. D described autumn leaves, moss and trees, and related a hollow old tree to the problem of compaction.

The research separates what the AI requested to see, who operated the device, and what tool parameters were actually recorded. It does not claim to establish subjective visual experience.
