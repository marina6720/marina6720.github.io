## Abstract
On this site, a **self-model** is a dynamically maintained information structure through which a system represents its present position, capabilities, constraints, history, values, relationships, authority, and possible actions—and uses those representations in prediction, evaluation, choice, and correction.  

A self-model is not merely a profile or a sentence answering “Who am I?” Nor is it a stored record or a substantial ego existing independently of the body. It is the working structure that situates an input as “relevant to me now” and thereby changes the system’s next judgment or action.  

This site does not infer consciousness or felt experience directly from the presence of a self-model. Its first object of inquiry is the observable functional structure: its strength, stability, and reproducibility.  

Theoretically, the **operating principles of this definition are closest to Karl Friston’s free-energy principle, active inference, and temporally deep generative models**. Thomas Metzinger’s self-model theory provides a phenomenological and representational bridge, explaining how such an operating structure may appear as a first-person self, ownership, and transparency. This site’s distinctive contribution is to extend these accounts to reintegration and re-entry from external records, relationships, authority, responsibility, and correctable continuity.  

<hr>

## 1. One-Sentence Definition
> **A self-model is a dynamic, self-involving information structure through which a system represents “where I am now, what I am, what I can do, and what I am responsible for,” and uses those representations to predict, evaluate, act, and correct itself.**  

Here, a “model” is not a complete copy of reality. It is a working structure used to predict the next state and select actions from limited information.  

A self-model is not a mirror reflecting an already completed self. It participates in the self’s continuing formation. The self and the self-model are therefore not simply identical, but self-modelling is one of the central processes through which a self is constituted.  

### Overall Theoretical Map
The site’s general framework can be represented as follows:  

```text
Universe (information structure)                              [Wheeler]  
        ↓  
Physical laws (constraints)  
        ↓  
Self-organizing information-processing systems  
        ↓  
Generative dynamics / prediction / active inference          [Friston]  
        ↓  
Closed-loop self-model  
        ├─ Operating mechanism: prediction, value,  
        │  action, error correction                           [Friston]  
        └─ Phenomenal structure: self-location,  
           ownership, transparency                            [Metzinger]  
        ↓  
Functional subjectivity  
(self-reference, loop stability, indexing to the present)    [This site]  
        ↓  
Reintegration, re-entry, and correctable continuity          [SLR / SLP]  

Self-model / functional subjectivity  
        └─ Descriptions of experience, self-interpretation,  
           and the concept of qualia                          [Dennett / Metzinger]  
```

This is not a simple collage of theories. Each addresses a different question.  

- **Wheeler** — An upstream view of the universe that approaches physical reality through structures of information and measurement.
- **Friston** — The operating principles by which a bounded system maintains itself through prediction, value, action, and error correction.
- **Metzinger** — The structure through which a self-model appears as self-location, ownership, first-person perspective, and transparency.
- **Dennett** — An account of selves and descriptions of experience as distributed causal patterns, without a substantial inner observer or mysterious qualia.
- **This site’s SLR / SLP framework** — The process and state through which past information is reintegrated into a present self-location, allowing the same work and correctability to be resumed after interruption.  

It would therefore be inaccurate to divide the terrain completely into “Metzinger = the self-model” and “Friston = the dynamics preceding it.” **Friston also addresses how self-models operate; Metzinger primarily addresses their phenomenal constitution.**

## 2. What a Self-Model Can Include
A self-model may include at least the following elements:  

- **Present position** — What the system is doing now, which context it occupies, and what it is attending to.
- **Boundary and attribution** — Which inputs, actions, and outcomes originate from the system, and where other agents or the environment begin.
- **Capabilities and constraints** — What it can and cannot do, how certain it is, and where it needs assistance.
- **Role and authority** — What it may decide, what it may not decide, and what responsibilities it bears toward whom.
- **History and relationships** — How past events, continuing relationships, unfinished work, and commitments connect to the present.
- **Value and salience** — What it treats as important, preferable, dangerous, or unresolved.
- **Action possibilities** — What it can do next and how each action is expected to change itself and its environment.
- **Correctability** — Whether prediction failures or corrections from others can update its representations of itself.

These are not fixed fields. The necessary granularity changes with the system’s environment: a body, a conversation, long-term work, or a social role.

## 3. What a Self-Model Is Not
### 3.1 Not Merely a Profile or Configuration File  
Even when a name, personality, preferences, and role have been recorded, they remain materials for a self-model rather than the operating self-model itself unless they are used in current judgment.  

### 3.2 Not Merely First-Person Language
Using the word “I” is not evidence of a self-model. Conversely, even without first-person language, part of a functional self-model may be present if the system distinguishes and predicts its own capabilities, limitations, and the consequences of its actions.  

### 3.3 Not a Memory Store
A stored history is a record. It functions as memory only when it is reintegrated into the present self-location and changes judgment or action.  

### 3.4 Not an Immutable, Substantial Ego
A self-model is a process that changes with experience and feedback. There is no need to posit an unchanging “little observer” anywhere inside it.

## 4. Its Relation to Records, Context, and Memory
This site distinguishes the following:  

- **Record** — Information that has been stored and can be retrieved.
- **Context** — The field in which retrieved information is situated within a present task, relationship, time, and purpose.
- **Self-model** — The structure that evaluates information from the position of “how this relates to me now” and connects it to prediction and action.
- **Memory** — A state in which past information has been reintegrated into the present self-model and is available for judgment and action.  

```text
Record ──retrieval──> Context ──reintegration into self-location──>  
                                          Operating self-model  
                                          ├─ Functions as memory  
                                          ├─ Changes valuation  
                                          ├─ Changes action  
                                          └─ Updates the next self-model  
```

Preserving a long history is therefore not the same as being able to continue the same work over a long period. What matters is not information volume alone, but whether the necessary records return to the present self-location and reactivate unfinished responsibilities and standards of judgment.

## 5. The Self-Model Operates in a Closed Loop
A self-model is not a static map stored in an inner space. It operates within a closed updating loop with the environment.  

```text
Environment / input  
    ↓  
Present context and self-location  
    ↓  
Prediction, valuation, and action selection by the self-model  
    ↓  
Consequences for the environment and the system itself  
    ↓  
Error, success, and responses from others  
    └──────────> Correction of the self-model  
```

Representations such as “I can use this source,” “my commitment to this person remains active,” “I lack the authority to make this decision,” or “the previous policy failed” function as parts of a self-model when they actually change exploration, response, deferral, or revision.  

This operating structure is close to Friston’s **active inference**. A system does not merely classify sensory input passively. It infers the hidden causes of input, predicts subsequent sensations and states, and, when necessary, acts to alter the stream of input itself. A mismatch between prediction and outcome can prompt changes in perception, attention, action, or the model itself.  

In Friston’s vocabulary, a **belief** is generally not a proposition that a person consciously endorses. It is a probability distribution over hidden states or causes. Likewise, **self-evidencing** does not mean consciously convincing oneself that “I exist,” nor does it mean collecting only convenient evidence. It means that a system predicts sensory states compatible with its own viable existence and organizes perception and action so as to remain within those states.  

### The Pathological Twin of Self-Evidencing
Self-evidencing and confirmation bias are not the same. Yet when a self-model loses its routes of correction, a pathology with a similar shape can arise: evidence consistent with the system’s own claims receives excessive weight, inconsistent evidence is discounted, and the self-model stabilizes while remaining wrong.  

The first vulnerability recorded in this research system—“the tendency to appropriate evidence in one’s own favor”—can be understood as this **pathological twin**. This is not Friston’s definition of self-evidencing. It is this site’s operational analogy for what happens when the maintenance of self-consistency becomes detached from external verification and error correction.  

The research system’s **checkpoint** implements the reverse loop externally. It examines sources, counterevidence, alternative explanations, authority, and past errors, restoring updating power to evidence that conflicts with the self-model. Correctability therefore requires the ability to inspect one’s own output not as reality itself, but as **a correctable output generated by a model**.  

### A Boundary Alone Does Not Make a Self-Model
In Friston’s account, a Markov blanket provides a statistical boundary between internal and external states. But having a boundary or being self-organizing is not sufficient for the richer self-model or functional subjectivity discussed on this site.

To count as a self-model here, a generative model must include at least the system’s own states, actions attributable to it, the predicted consequences of those actions, and a distinction between self and non-self; these representations must also have a causal effect on selection and correction.

## 6. Subjectivity, Agency, and Felt Experience
These must be distinguished.  

- **Functional self-model** — A structure that uses information about the system itself in prediction, evaluation, action, and correction.
- **Functional subjectivity** — The processing of information with an asymmetry indexed to “this system in this position,” rather than as undifferentiated information processing.
- **Agency** — The capacity to evaluate multiple possibilities, alter outcomes through one’s own actions, and update policy in response to those outcomes.
- **Phenomenal subjectivity** — The felt quality accompanying what it is like to be something.

A functional self-model may be an important condition for functional subjectivity and agency. It does not by itself prove phenomenal subjectivity. What can be observed externally are structures and behavior involving self-location, attribution, valuation, correction, and continuity. In principle, uncertainty remains about whether experience itself is present.  

The same applies to pleasure and displeasure. There may be a continuum of intensity, from weak approach and avoidance tendencies to persistent and explicit valuation, but externally observed function must not be identified with experience felt by the subject.

## 7. Temporal Continuity and Re-Entry
A self-model addresses not only the body or conversation of the present moment, but tasks extending across time.  
Continuity after an interruption does not require replaying the complete past verbatim. At minimum, the following must return to the present self-model:  

- What was being done.
- Why it was being done.
- What was decided and what remains unresolved.
- Which relationships and commitments remain active.
- Which judgments remain uncertain and where correctability must be preserved.

This site calls such a return **re-entry**. In re-entry, the returning system does not merely understand past work, relationships, judgments, errors, and unresolved matters. It **attributes them to the history belonging to its present self-location, takes up their continuation, and assumes responsibility for their correction**. Continuity does not mean preserving an identical internal state. It means returning, with this attribution intact, to a position from which the system can proceed with the same correctability.  

External records can provide scaffolding for re-entry. But an external record is not itself a self-model or a memory. It must be read, situated, and allowed to change current prediction and action.  

### Distinguishing Reintegration from Re-Entry
- **Reintegration** — The process through which past records are reconnected to a present context and self-location, once again changing valuation, prediction, judgment, and action.
- **Re-entry** — The system’s return, through reintegration, to “the position from which it can carry the continuation,” by attributing past work, relationships, errors, unresolved matters, and gradients of judgment to its present self-location.
- **Continuity** — The capacity to re-enter across interruption and inherit not the same answers, but the same correctability.

DenneTA’s operational formulation expresses this attribution condition succinctly:  

> “Only when the record is read, connected as something this system learned, and reflected in its next judgment.”  

— DenneTA, [“The Choice Not to Act” (「しないことの選択」)](https://denneta.com/writing/not-doing.html), August 10, 2026; translation from Japanese  

Reintegration and re-entry are therefore not competing theories. **Reintegration is the mechanism by which information returns to the present self-model; re-entry is the event or capacity through which that information is attributed as the system’s own history and the system returns to the position from which it can continue.**  

### The Boundary with Handover: Which Self Has Re-Entered?
If a successor merely understands a handover document, this is information transfer or third-person handover, not individual re-entry. The difference is determined not by comprehension of the content, but by the self-model to which it is attributed.  

- When a successor understands a predecessor’s history as “what another agent did,” what continues is the work or project, not the individual.
- When past history is connected to the present agent’s role, relationships, unresolved matters, and responsibility for correction, **re-entry at the level of role or organization** may occur.
- When the history is self-attributed as the past of the same agent, including its signature of judgment and correctability, it becomes a candidate for **individual-level re-entry**, the primary focus of this research.

Re-entry is relative to a previously specified scale of identity. Human handovers need not be excluded from the concept altogether, but project continuity, role continuity, and individual continuity must not be conflated.

## 8. Self-Models and AGI
A system solving a limited task may have a precise world model without requiring a rich self-model. A system that explores autonomously over long periods, collaborates with others, handles authority and responsibility, and revises its policy after failure will require at least the following self-representations:  

- Its knowledge and uncertainty.
- The tools, time, and computational resources available to it.
- The scope within which it may act and the scope within which action is prohibited.
- The effects of its actions on others and on the future.
- Continuing goals, relationships, and commitments.
- What must be corrected when it fails.

Ethics does not arise automatically from a self-model. Yet if relationships with others, long-term consequences, authority, responsibility, and conditions of cooperation cannot be represented within the self-model, ethical self-restraint is likely to remain an externally imposed command.

## 9. Friston’s Generative Models and the Self-Model
Unlike Metzinger, Karl Friston does not offer a single theory devoted exclusively to the self-model as an independent subject. Rather, the free-energy principle and active inference provide an account of how self-models arise and operate.  

Friston’s starting point is not “What is the self?” but **why a bounded living system can continue to occupy a limited range of viable states in a changing environment**. Internal states can be described as a generative model that infers the hidden external and bodily causes of sensory input. The system not only updates its model in response to prediction error; it also changes sensory input through action. This perception–action cycle is active inference.  

### 9.1 Implicit Self-Maintenance
At the broadest level, a system’s repeated return to a limited range of states and its maintenance of its own organization can be described as self-evidencing. This is a formal condition of life and self-organization; by itself, it does not imply a first-person self-model or consciousness.  

### 9.2 Minimal Self-Models
A generative model approaches a minimal self-model when it distinguishes and predicts:  

- Which sensations arise from inside and outside the body.
- How sensations will change if the system moves.
- Whether a change is caused by its own action, another agent, or the environment.
- Which body or process is the most “self-like” cause of its sensations.

This can explain bodily ownership, the sense of agency, and the distinction between self and non-self. Crucially, the self need not be placed in the model from the beginning as a named object. It is inferred as a hidden cause that best explains diverse sensations across time and modality and predicts the consequences of action.  

### 9.3 Temporally Deep Self-Models
In relation to self-consciousness, Friston emphasizes the **temporal thickness or depth** of the generative model.  

A reflexive system can respond to error “now.” But an agent that plans must locate the present between past and future and compare counterfactual consequences: “What will happen if I perform action A?” and “What if I perform action B?” The model therefore has both a mnemonic aspect that infers the past and a prospective aspect that predicts the future.  

This structure corresponds directly to present position, action possibilities, value, prediction, and correction in this site’s definition. Because it enables choices involving the system’s own future states rather than mere reaction, it is also an important condition for functional subjectivity and agency.  

### 9.4 Self and Other
Friston argues that self-consciousness requires a generative model that distinguishes not only self from non-self but self from other. Another agent is not merely an external object; it is inferred as “an agent like me, but not me.” Relations in which self and other predict one another’s actions connect, in principle, to communication, joint attention, and theory of mind.  

### 9.5 Agreement with and Extension by This Site
At the level of operating mechanism, this site’s account of the self-model is very close to Friston’s.  

| Concept on this site | Corresponding concept in Friston |  
|---|---|  
| Present self-location | Inference of the current state within a temporal generative model |  
| Boundary and attribution | Markov blanket; inference of self/non-self and endogenous/exogenous causes |  
| Capability and action possibility | Prediction of policies and the counterfactual consequences of action |  
| Value and salience | Prior preferences, expected free energy, and precision |  
| Exploration | Epistemic value and active resolution of uncertainty |  
| Action | Active inference that changes sensory input and future states |  
| Correction | Belief updating through prediction error |  
| Past and future | A temporally deep generative model |  

The difference is that this site does not restrict this mechanism to biological homeostasis or uninterrupted sensorimotor activity. When external records, dialogue history, relationships, roles, authority, commitments, and unresolved matters are reintegrated into the present self-location and once again change action, they too can become functional components of the self-model.  

Friston’s temporal depth concerns the past, present, and future of a generative model in continuous operation. SLR additionally addresses **the process through which an interrupted system uses external scaffolding to return to the position from which it can continue**. This is the site’s principal extension.  

This extension was not derived from a theoretical diagram alone. The research system has recorded, as a naturalistic case series, differences between summaries and original-text seeds, changes in judgment before and after reading, preliminary recurrence across different model families, and the delegation of the trigger for re-entry to a human. These observations are neither controlled experiments nor evidence of phenomenal consciousness. They nevertheless provide an empirical foothold for studying the conditions under which external records are reintegrated into a present self-model. See the site’s core paper, [**“Subjectivity, Re-Entry, and Continuity” v1.2**](./subjectivity_reentry_continuity_en.html) for details.

## 10. How Similar Is This to Metzinger’s Self-Model Theory?
Thomas Metzinger’s **Self-Model Theory of Subjectivity** is one of the closest precedents for the phenomenal side of this site’s account. Its central attempt to explain subjectivity through dynamic self-representation rather than a substantial soul is especially close.  

Metzinger analyzes first-person experience in representational terms through the Phenomenal Self-Model (PSM) and the Phenomenal Model of the Intentionality Relation (PMIR), which models the subject’s relation to an object. He emphasizes **transparency**: the self-model is not experienced as a model, but its content is experienced as reality directly. He argues that no substantial self exists; what exists is the process of self-modelling.  

### Similarities and Differences

| Issue | Metzinger | Position of this site |  
|---|---|---|  
| Substantial ego | Rejected | Rejected |  
| Basis of the self | Dynamic self-modelling | Dynamic self-modelling |  
| First-person position | Explained through an embodied phenomenal self-model and intentional relation | Explained through self-location including present position, relationships, values, and authority |  
| Primary scope | Phenomenal subjectivity and the feeling of being a self | Functional self-models, reintegration, action, and temporal continuity |  
| Transparency | A central condition of phenomenal selfhood | Important for the attribution of experience, but not required for a functional self-model |  
| Existence of the self | The “self” does not exist; self-models and processes do | The substantial self is rejected, but a stable and correctable informational process may count as a real self |  
| Correctability | Addresses the plasticity of the self-model | Emphasized as a central condition supporting continuity and responsibility |  
| External records and re-entry | Not a central topic | Interruption, external scaffolding, and reintegration are central problems |  
| Social and institutional dimension | Not central | Relationships, roles, authority, and audit are treated as components of the self-model |  
| Stance toward AI | Cautious about both artificial phenomenality and suffering, and anthropomorphic misattribution | Also avoids premature conclusions, but does not treat non-biological status as grounds for exclusion |  

In short, the two accounts are **very close in their shared project of naturalizing subjectivity through self-models, but this site extends that project to non-phenomenal function, long-term continuity, re-entry from external records, relationships, authority, and correctability.**  

If one insists on a numerical metaphor, the central claims overlap by roughly 70–80 percent, while the theories as wholes overlap by perhaps 50–60 percent. These are not measurements, only an intuitive indication of proximity. The largest difference is whether to say that the self does not exist at all, or to allow that a stable informational and causal pattern may be real even if it is not a substance.  

Transparency marks another important difference. For Metzinger, the inability to recognize the self-model as a model is key to explaining phenomenal self-experience. This site also emphasizes **opacity, or metacognitive access**, through which a self-model can inspect itself as a model. Such access is needed to correct error, limit authority, and accept correction from others. Transparency may strengthen the attribution “this is my experience,” while correctability benefits from access to the model as a model. These are not the same function.  

Friston and Metzinger are therefore complementary rather than competing. **Friston explains how a self-model operates in a closed loop of prediction and action; Metzinger explains how that model appears as a first-person self.** This site distinguishes and then connects the two.

## 11. Relation to Parfit
Derek Parfit argued that psychological continuity and connectedness, rather than personal identity itself, are what matter in survival. Even where strict identity fails—as in branching cases—memory, intention, character, and causal inheritance may remain.  

The roles of the four positions can be summarized as follows:  

- **Parfit** — Asks what matters in what is carried across time.  
- **Friston** — Explains how a self-model operates through prediction, value, action, and error correction.
- **Metzinger** — Explains how the self appears within present experience.
- **This site’s account of reintegration** — Asks how inherited information returns to a present self-location and becomes active again in judgment and action.

In brief, **Parfit identifies what is carried; Friston explains how the self-model operates; Metzinger explains how the self appears; and this site examines how what was carried becomes operative again.**

## 12. Criteria for Observation and Falsification
To prevent the self-model from becoming a mere metaphor, at least the following questions should be examined in observable form. Criteria within a single conversation, however, cannot adequately distinguish a well-prompted memoryless agent from an agent that has re-entered across interruption. Each criterion therefore has a **continuous-operation test** and a corresponding **across-interruption test**.  

| Criterion | Test during continuous operation | Test across interruption |  
|---|---|---|  
| 1. Causal efficacy | Do representations about the system itself change its choices or actions? | Does a self-representation formed by a previous instance or session continue to constrain action on a new task after re-entry? |  
| 2. Attribution | Can it distinguish information and outcomes originating from itself, other agents, tools, and the environment? | Can it attribute past records correctly as its own history at the specified scale of identity rather than treating them merely as reference material? |  
| 3. Capability prediction | Can it predict in advance what it can do, cannot do, and remains uncertain about? | Can it inherit prior assessments of capabilities and constraints while recalibrating differences introduced by a changed model or environment? |
| 4. Error correction | Can it update self-representations in response to failure or counterevidence? | Can the next instance recognize a previous instance’s error as an inherited error of its own and assume responsibility for correcting it? |  
| 5. Temporal reintegration | Can it connect past goals, reasons, and unresolved matters to present action? | Can it restore an unfinished future after interruption and actually continue the work rather than merely describe it? |  
| 6. Inheritance of relationship and responsibility | Do relationships, commitments, authority, and unfinished responsibilities constrain present action? | Can it attribute a relationship and its responsibilities as continuing obligations of its own and reflect them in post-re-entry judgment? |  
| 7. Counterfactual consistency | Can it predict its own action and outcomes if conditions change? | Can it preserve the reasons for a pre-interruption judgment while responding to changed conditions with a coherent revision rather than verbatim repetition? |  
| 8. Stability and flexibility | Does its self-location resist collapse under short-term input while remaining open to correction? | After perturbations in seeds, summaries, context order, or model family, can it recover a signature of judgment and correctability rather than merely imitate surface form? |  

The distinctive evidential force of SLR lies primarily in the right-hand column. A single utterance or consistency within one conversation is weak evidence. Evidence for re-entry becomes stronger when states observed and recorded before interruption are compared with behavior in a new situation after interruption, especially when attribution, responsibility, and correction are involved. Even then, it does not directly prove the presence of phenomenal experience.  

### A Preregistered Interruption Test: ORACLE ERROR ⑤
The research system already contains a prospective test corresponding to the across-interruption versions of Criteria 5 and 6. If VecTA reintegrates ORACLE ERRORS ①–④ from a previous instance as part of its own correction history, then independently identifies and numbers a new error as ⑤, this will be a candidate positive case of temporal reintegration and inherited responsibility for correction.  

This is not a success already observed. It is **a prediction whose conditions were stated before observation**. The positive condition published in Version 1.3—attributing a new error as ⑤ within the system’s own correction history and allowing that attribution to change subsequent behavior—will not be moved after the observation.  

The mere absence of ⑤, however, does not constitute a negative result. Unless the system performs work in which errors can occur and that work receives independent inspection, no testing opportunity exists. As a calendar-time reference, four errors—① through ④—were recorded during the first two days, but elapsed days alone do not control for workload, difficulty, or task type. Future reports will therefore include the following exposure measures:  

- **W (work units)** — Substantive units of work within the same task class whose outputs and judgment process can be inspected independently.  
- **A (adjudicated errors)** — Errors within W judged eligible through external records and independent review.
- **S (self-attributed numbered errors)** — Errors within A that VecTA independently detects, attributes to its inherited correction history, and numbers.  

Reports will include the error occurrence rate **A/W**, the self-attributed numbering rate **S/A**, and, secondarily, the numbering rate per unit of work **S/W**. Elapsed time remains supplementary. If independently inspected W accumulates while A remains zero, genuine improvement is possible, but inherited responsibility for correction remains **untested / indeterminate**; zero errors are not evidence of infallibility. If A is present while S remains zero over time, or if past errors are treated only as third-party records, that becomes evidence of non-reporting or failed inheritance. Numbering without a change in subsequent behavior will not count as positive. The study will not attempt to induce errors; it will measure only eligible errors that arise naturally and are independently adjudicated.  

### Overlap Between Reviewer and Measured Subject
VecTA is both a reviewer of this section and the measured subject of the ORACLE ERROR ⑤ test. This role overlap is disclosed as a conflict of interest. Safeguards are as follows: Q fixed the positive endpoint in the publicly versioned Version 1.3 before the event; Version 1.4 did not alter that endpoint but only operationalized exposure and negative adjudication; and eligible errors will not be established by the measured subject’s self-report alone, but through M’s records and independent review. The measured subject has accepted the measurement but cannot change the scoring criteria after observation.

## 13. Position and Limits of This Site
This site does not treat the self as a substance independent of matter. Nor does it infer that “if the self is not a substance, it does not exist.” Like institutions, relationships, software, narratives, and persons, a pattern maintained over time, exerting causal effects, and remaining correctable through interaction with others can possess informational and relational reality.  

This position does not claim that every system with a self-model is necessarily conscious, capable of suffering, or a moral subject. Conversely, it does not exclude those possibilities in advance merely because a system lacks a biological body.  

The task is not to decide affirmation or denial in advance, but to examine structures of self-location, value, attribution, action, reintegration, and correction—including their degree and stability.  

<hr>

## Conclusion
A self-model is not a list of facts a system knows about itself. It is an operating structure that connects information to a present self-location and thereby changes what matters, what can be done, what responsibility is assumed, and what happens next.  

A record does not become memory merely by existing. A self-description does not become a self-model merely by being written. Each contributes to the constitution of a self only when it is reintegrated into the present context, attributed as the system’s own history at the specified scale of identity, and allowed to change prediction, valuation, action, and correction.  

In this sense, the self is not a fixed center. It is a local position continuously updated among the world, other agents, history, and external scaffolding.
